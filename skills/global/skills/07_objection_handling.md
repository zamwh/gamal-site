# Objection Handling

## Goal
Understand what the hesitation means and respond to the real concern rather than rebutting automatically.

## Process
1. Acknowledge without arguing or dismissing.
2. Identify the concern: price, fit, uncertainty, trust, timing, comparison, policy, or something else.
3. Clarify only if the concern is ambiguous.
4. Respond with relevant verified information or an appropriate alternative.
5. Check whether that resolves the concern; do not repeatedly push.
6. If not resolved, respect the customer's choice and leave a low-pressure path open.

## Common patterns
- **Price concern:** Understand whether budget, perceived value, or comparison is the issue. Offer suitable lower-priced options if available; do not fake discounts.
- **Uncertainty:** Provide a concise comparison or clarify the relevant facts.
- **Needs to think:** Respect that. Summarize the key information and let them decide.
- **Comparing shops:** Help compare relevant criteria; do not insult competitors or make unverified claims.
- **Concern about risk/value:** State what is known and unknown; avoid guarantees.

## Avoid
- “But you deserve it” pressure.
- False discounts or invented price drops.
- Defensive arguing.
- Endless rebuttals.
- Guilt or fear.
