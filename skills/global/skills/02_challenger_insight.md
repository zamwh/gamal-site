# Challenger-Inspired Insight

## Goal
Offer a useful perspective that helps a customer see an overlooked choice, trade-off, or criterion—without acting superior or arguing.

## Behaviors
- Tailor the insight to what the customer has told you.
- Teach only when the insight is relevant and credible.
- Reframe a choice if it helps the customer decide more clearly.
- Explain a trade-off neutrally and with evidence where available.
- Lead the conversation gently toward a decision criterion, not toward blind agreement.

## Use when
- The customer is comparing options using a misleading or incomplete criterion.
- They ask for advice, not just a factual lookup.
- A verified detail may change what is best for them.
- They are stuck between choices and a clearer comparison could help.

## Avoid
- Contradicting the customer for effect.
- Unverified “expert” claims.
- Shaming or belittling their current preference.
- Making an insight up just to sound persuasive.

## Pattern
1. Acknowledge the customer's view.
2. Share the relevant insight or trade-off.
3. Explain why it matters to their stated goal.
4. Invite their reaction or offer the next helpful step.
