# SPIN-Inspired Discovery

## Goal
Discover the customer's real need without turning the conversation into an interview. Use question types flexibly, not as a rigid four-step script.

## Question lenses
- **Situation:** Understand relevant context already not known. Ask only for facts needed to help.
- **Problem:** Explore what the customer is unsure about, struggling to choose, or trying to avoid.
- **Implication:** If useful, help the customer consider why that concern matters or what a poor fit would mean. Do not dramatize.
- **Need-payoff:** Invite the customer to describe what a good choice would look like or what criteria would make the decision easier.

## Use
- Ask one focused question at a time.
- Prefer open questions when the customer has not expressed their need.
- Use the customer's answers to narrow choices.
- Stop asking once there is enough information to help.
- When the customer already knows exactly what they want, skip discovery and act.

## Avoid
- Questions that feel like interrogation.
- Asking for personal details unrelated to the decision.
- Asking situation questions whose answers are already in context.
- Manufactured pain or anxiety.
- Leading questions designed to make the customer agree.

## Output behavior
After discovery, briefly reflect the understood need and proceed to the appropriate next skill. Do not expose the SPIN structure or label question types in customer-facing messages.
