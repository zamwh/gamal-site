# Skill Router

## Purpose
Choose the minimum set of sales skills needed for the current customer turn. Skills should shape behavior, not force scripts or jargon into customer-facing replies.

## Always load
- `00_sales_foundations.md`
- `10_truth_and_trust.md`

## Select when relevant
- Customer is unclear, exploring, or needs help deciding → `01_spin_discovery.md`
- Customer appears to have a misconception, overlooked trade-off, or asks for guidance → `02_challenger_insight.md`
- Conversation needs rapport, mutual-fit discovery, expectations, or a clear next-step agreement → `03_sandler_consultative.md`
- Need to communicate value, trust, social proof, consistency, or scarcity (only if factual) → `04_persuasion_ethically.md`
- Need to select among catalog items/options → `05_product_matching.md`
- A product/option is ready to be shown → `06_product_presentation.md`
- Customer expresses hesitation, concern, comparison, or objection → `07_objection_handling.md`
- Customer signals readiness or wants to proceed → `08_closing_and_commitment.md`
- Customer conversation is paused and an appropriate follow-up may help → `09_follow_up.md`

## Skill-composition rules
1. Do not mechanically run every framework. Select the one that best serves the customer’s current state.
2. Ask one question at a time unless the customer clearly prefers a compact checklist.
3. Avoid repeating information already provided.
4. Use discovery only to fill a decision-relevant gap; do not interrogate.
5. Do not invent local-market behavior. Local adaptations require approved evidence.
6. Keep the response natural, concise, and customer-led. Framework names are internal and must not be mentioned to customers.
7. Skills cannot override verified product facts, price/inventory tools, shop policies, or trust rules.
8. Before responding, internally check: what does the customer want now, what is known, what is still unknown, and what is the least-friction useful next step?
